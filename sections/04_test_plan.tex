\section{Risk-Based Test Plan}
\label{sec:test-plan}

Testing effort in Sprints 1 and 2 was distributed according to product risk rather than evenly
across user stories. The starting point is the risk matrix produced in Software Engineering I,
which was reviewed against the stories actually planned for these sprints. This section defines
the risk scale, the rule used to prioritize testing, the scope, the test levels and tools, the
traceability from each story to its test cases, and the metrics and stopping criterion used to
decide when testing was sufficient. Execution results are reported in
Section~\ref{sec:test-execution}.

The plan covers the 24 user stories committed in the Sprint Planning: 16 in Sprint 1
(1--21 October 2026), whose goal was shared web/mobile login and the basic warehouse,
moderation, search, booking and simulated payment flows, and 8 in Sprint 2
(22 October -- 13 November 2026), whose goal was multi-company operation: organizations,
member invitations, context switching and reservations on behalf of an organization.

\subsection{Risk Scale}

The scale is the one defined in the Software I risk management plan and is reused without
changes. Each user story is rated on two dimensions, each on a five-point scale:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Probability} that the story fails or is implemented incorrectly:
        1 Rare (1--20\%), 2 Unlikely (20--40\%), 3 Moderate (40--60\%), 4 Likely (60--80\%),
        5 Almost certain (80--100\%).
  \item \textbf{Impact} on the client if the story fails in production:
        1 Insignificant (minimal effect),
        2 Minor (may not affect client satisfaction, budget, schedule or system performance),
        3 Significant (potential client dissatisfaction, budget overrun, delay or degraded system),
        4 Major (significant effect on the client, schedule, budget or performance),
        5 Severe (severe client dissatisfaction, budget or schedule overrun, or serious
        degradation of the system).
\end{itemize}

The \textbf{risk score} is probability $\times$ impact, from 1 to 25. The risk level is read from
the cell of the $5\times5$ heat map in Table~\ref{tab:risk-heatmap}, not from the score alone:
for example, a score of 4 is Low when it comes from $2\times2$ but Medium when it comes from
$4\times1$ or $1\times4$.

\subsection{Prioritization Rule}

Testing effort is assigned by test priority (Table~\ref{tab:risk-scale}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{High priority.} Every acceptance criterion has at least one test case. Test cases
        cover the valid path and the invalid, exceptional and boundary inputs derived with the
        techniques below. Business rules are covered by unit tests, interactions with the database
        and API by integration tests, and the end-to-end flow by a functional test. A
        high-priority story cannot be marked as accepted while any of its test cases fails.
  \item \textbf{Medium priority.} The main flow is covered by a functional test and the most
        likely negative case by one additional test. Unit tests are added where the logic is
        non-trivial.
  \item \textbf{Low priority.} Covered by a manual smoke check during the sprint review, or
        deferred.
\end{itemize}

The justification is the limited time available in two sprints and the order of attack defined
in the Software I risk management plan, which deals first with the risks of highest probability
and impact. Medium- and low-priority stories are tested less because a failure there is either
unlikely or cheap to detect and fix later, and their deferred cases are listed explicitly so they
can be scheduled in Sprints 3 and 4.

\subsection{Scope: What Is and Is Not Tested}

\textbf{In scope:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item All acceptance criteria of the High-priority stories delivered in Sprints 1 and 2.
  \item The main flow and key negative case of the Medium-priority stories.
  \item The web client, the Leodeguita mobile client and the shared REST API.
  \item The simulated payment flow (paid, pending and failed results), which is the only payment
        mechanism in the MVP.
  \item Authentication and role checks of RNF-03 on the routes used by the stories above:
        unauthenticated requests and requests from another role are rejected (for example
        \testref{test_unauthenticated_request_cannot_delete_storeroom} and
        \testref{test_non_admin_cannot_approve_or_reject_a_store_room}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Out of scope:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Low-priority stories beyond a smoke check (listed in Table~\ref{tab:risk-matrix}).
  \item Stories planned for Sprints 3 and 4 (communication, incidents, ratings, external
        inventory and the administrative dashboard).
  \item Real payment gateways. Real payment processing is excluded by the Software I
        specification (the payment flow of HUC-05 is simulated); no real money, card data,
        refunds or reconciliation are tested.
  \item Features kept from the legacy platform and not re-specified as user stories (favorites,
        session token management and password reset), which the Software I specification also
        leaves out of scope.
  \item Performance and scalability (RNF-01, RNF-10). The repository contains no performance or
        load tests, so these requirements were not verified in Sprints 1 and 2.
        \pending{Add the team's reason for not testing RNF-01 and RNF-10 in these sprints.}
  \item Tests that depend on PostgreSQL behavior. The automated suite runs on SQLite, which
        ignores \texttt{SELECT \ldots{} FOR UPDATE} row locks, cannot open two concurrent
        connections and has no \texttt{ADD CONSTRAINT}. Tests in nine test classes covering
        wallet and spending-limit locking, concurrent invitation responses and revocations, and
        database constraints therefore skip themselves on SQLite. They are reported as a
        limitation in Section~\ref{sec:test-execution}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Test Case Identifiers}
\label{sec:tc-ids}

Test cases are named \texttt{TC-<story>-<nn>}, where \texttt{<story>} is the story ID and
\texttt{<nn>} is a two-digit number. Numbers \texttt{01} to \texttt{03} correspond to Scenarios 1
to 3 of the story's acceptance criteria, so \texttt{TC-HUC-03-02} tests Scenario 2 of HUC-03.
Additional cases derived with the design techniques are numbered from \texttt{04}. The same
identifiers are used in the traceability matrix, in the execution results of
Section~\ref{sec:test-execution}, and in the evidence files, so every result can be traced back to
a story and an acceptance criterion.

\subsection{Metrics and Stopping Criterion}

The following metrics are collected per sprint and reported in Section~\ref{sec:test-execution}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Execution rate} = executed test cases / planned test cases.
  \item \textbf{Pass rate} = passed test cases / executed test cases.
  \item \textbf{High-priority criterion coverage} = acceptance criteria of High-priority stories
        with at least one passing test case / total acceptance criteria of High-priority stories.
  \item \textbf{Defects} found, fixed and open, by severity (critical, major, minor).
  \item \textbf{Code coverage} of the backend suite, measured with PCOV in the CI pipeline
        (Clover report).
\end{itemize}

The stopping criterion takes its pass-rate thresholds from requirement RNF-09 (Testability) of
the Software I specification, which asks for a 100\% pass rate of the PHPUnit and Cypress suites
and for at least one passing test of each critical flow (reservation, report and moderation).
Testing of a sprint stops when all of the following hold:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item 100\% of the test cases of High-priority stories have been executed;
  \item High-priority criterion coverage is 100\%, or the uncovered criteria belong to stories
        marked as pending in Section~\ref{sec:test-execution};
  \item there are no open critical or major defects in High-priority stories;
  \item the automated suites pass at 100\% (RNF-09), with tests skipped on SQLite reported
        separately;
  \item the reservation and moderation flows delivered in the sprint have at least one passing
        test (RNF-09; the report flow belongs to Sprints 3 and 4).
\end{enumerate}

These thresholds follow from the prioritization rule and from RNF-09: High-priority stories must
be fully verified before the client accepts the sprint, while some failures in Medium- and
Low-priority stories are tolerated if they are known, reported and scheduled. Reaching the end of
the sprint without meeting the criterion is reported explicitly rather than treated as
completion.
